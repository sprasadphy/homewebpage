Research

My research focuses on emergent collective behavior in QCD matter and on advanced analysis strategies for collider data, combining precision observables with machine-learning-assisted reconstruction and classification.

Anisotropic Flow and Initial-State Geometry

A key objective is to quantify how initial geometric anisotropies are mapped into final-state momentum anisotropies. I study the harmonic flow coefficients $v_n$ through multi-particle cumulants and differential analyses in transverse momentum and centrality.

The azimuthal particle distribution is commonly expressed as $$ \frac{dN}{d\varphi} \propto 1 + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} v_n \cos\left[n(\varphi - \Psi_n)\right], $$ where $\Psi_n$ denotes the event-plane angle of harmonic order $n$.

Jet and Two-Particle Correlations

I investigate near-side and away-side correlation structures to disentangle collective backgrounds from jet-induced signals. This includes studies of angular broadening, yield modifications, and path-length dependent quenching signatures.

Event-Shape Engineering and Topology

A central line of my work is event topology characterization using observables such as transverse spherocity and flattenicity. These techniques are used to classify final states and isolate underlying production mechanisms from soft-QCD to heavy-flavor sectors.

ALICE Collaboration Analyses

Flattenicity-Dependent Production

Particle production as a function of charged-particle flattenicity in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13$ TeV (published in Phys. Rev. D).

Strange Hadron Production

Study of $K^{0}_{S}$ and $\Lambda+\bar{\Lambda}$ versus charged-particle flattenicity in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=13.6$ TeV with ALICE (Analysis Note 1609, Preliminary Approved).

Dihadron Jet-Medium Correlations

Investigating jet-medium interaction via dihadron correlations in p-O collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=9.62$ TeV with ALICE (Analysis Note 1695, Preliminary Approved).

Methods and Analysis Infrastructure

My workflow combines robust event and track selection, systematic uncertainty propagation, and reproducible data-processing scripts suitable for collaboration scale analyses. Emphasis is placed on transparent documentation and portability of results across computing environments.

Programming and analysis stack includes C/C++, Python, ROOT, machine-learning workflows, and Monte Carlo/event generators such as PYTHIA8, AMPT, HIJING, EPOS(-LHC), MUSIC, IP-Glasma, and UrQMD.
